Right now the direction we are taking is to model in Rocq and LEAN the WP of why3. I looked at the litteratur and found that.

## The key existing work: Cohen & Johnson-Freyd (POPL 2024)

The `joscoh/why3-semantics` repo formalizes Why3's syntax, type system (including strict positivity for inductive predicates), and gives a denotational semantics for Why3 terms and formulas, interpreting them as objects in Coq's logic. It handles recursive types via W-types, algebraic datatypes with injectivity/disjointness proofs, and pattern matching.

The Why3 and EasyCrypt forks are up-to-date as of mid-February 2025, and Cohen's thesis (Princeton, May 2025) extends this toward a foundationally verified compiler for the Why3 IVL.

## Critical gap for your use case

This existing formalization covers **Why3's logic fragment** — not the WhyML program language or its WP/VCgen. Why3 consists of two parts: a rich logic for program specification, and an ML-like language WhyML with a built-in verification condition generator generating formulas in the underlying logic. The POPL 2024 paper focuses on the former.

So the situation for your proof is:

| Layer | Cohen & JF | You need |
|---|---|---|
| Why3 logic (terms, formulas, types) | ✅ covered | use directly |
| Denotational semantics of logic | ✅ covered | use directly |
| WhyML program semantics | ❌ not covered | build this |
| WP transformer (VCgen) | ❌ not covered | build this |
| PyCSL → WhyML translation | ❌ out of scope | you have this |

## Concrete recommendation

**Yes, we should build on Cohen & Johnson-Freyd**, for two reasons:

1. **Free semantic domain.** Their denotational semantics gives you the interpretation of Why3 formulas in Coq's logic directly — which is exactly the codomain of your WP transformer. You don't want to re-derive this.

2. **Alignment with actual Why3.** Since PyCSL compiles to real Why3, grounding your consistency proof in a formalization that tracks the actual Why3 implementation (via the forked OCaml source) is a strong argument against a gap between your model and the tool.

**What you'll still need to add** on top of it:

- A small-step or big-step semantics for the WhyML fragment that PyCSL emits (likely just: imperative core + `let`/`ref`/`while`/`assert`). You don't need all of WhyML — just the target subset.
- A WP transformer over that fragment, mapping to the Cohen/JF formula type.
- Your soundness theorem: if PyCSL's semantics satisfies the precondition, then the WP of the emitted WhyML w.r.t. the postcondition holds.

One thing worth checking: Cohen's May 2025 thesis ("A Foundationally Verified Intermediate Verification Language") may already include a verified VCgen on top of the POPL 2024 logic formalization — that would significantly reduce your work on the WP side. It's worth reading before you commit to modeling it yourself.
