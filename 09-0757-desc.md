# 09-0757-desc.md — the nature and origin of the `--deep` proof bloat

## What kind of proof this is, precisely

It is worth being exact about what "the os proof" means, because the nature of the cost follows
directly from it.

The os is annotated Python. The transpiler lowers it to **WhyML**, the input language of the **Why3**
platform. Why3 then performs **deductive verification**: from each WhyML program function and its
contract it computes **verification conditions** by a **weakest-precondition calculus** — first-order
logic formulas whose validity means "this function body satisfies its postcondition, calls every
callee within that callee's precondition, preserves every loop invariant, and accesses every array
in bounds." Why3 splits those formulas into independent sub-goals and hands each one to **SMT solvers**
— here Alt-Ergo 2.6.2, with Z3 4.13.3 as a fallback — under a fixed per-goal time limit (thirty
seconds). A sub-goal is discharged when a solver reports it *Valid* (equivalently, it found the
negation unsatisfiable); a sub-goal that no solver closes in its time budget comes back *Unknown* or
*Timeout* and is reported as remaining unproven. The twenty-three unproven goals are exactly such
sub-goals.

Three negatives matter as much as the positive:

- **It is not a Rocq (Coq) proof.** No Rocq kernel runs while the os is checked. Rocq — and Lean —
  appear only *offline*, to justify a small number of cited axioms; those axioms enter the WhyML as
  assumptions, but the act of checking the os is not a proof-assistant session and produces no proof
  term.
- **It is not propositional SAT.** A SAT solver decides satisfiability of Boolean formulas with no
  theories and no quantifiers. The verification conditions here are first-order, quantified, and laden
  with theories — integer arithmetic, the theory of arrays, and uninterpreted function symbols.
- **It is SMT — satisfiability *modulo theories*.** The solver does contain a Boolean SAT engine
  (a CDCL/DPLL core), but that core is wrapped by theory decision procedures and, decisively for this
  workload, by **quantifier instantiation** — trigger-based E-matching that picks ground terms to
  instantiate the universally-quantified hypotheses in scope. For these goals the dominant cost is not
  the propositional search; it is this quantifier instantiation against the quantified facts the goal
  can see.

So "the os proof normally finishes in fifteen to twenty minutes" means, precisely: Why3 generates the
weakest-precondition verification conditions, splits them, and Alt-Ergo (then Z3) discharges all but
twenty-three of the resulting sub-goals within their per-goal budgets, the whole sweep taking that much
wall-clock. With `--deep`, that sweep had not terminated past twenty-three minutes and was abandoned.

## What `--deep` changes in what the solver sees

Each verification condition is not solved in a vacuum. It is solved against the **logical context** of
the WhyML module: every type invariant, every declared symbol, every imported function's contract, and
every cited axiom that is in scope at that point. A function that the os merely imports contributes no
verification condition of its own — it appears as an abstract `val` with its contract attached — but
that contract becomes a **hypothesis in scope for every goal that can reach it**, and if the contract
is universally quantified (the codec's contracts quantify over byte indices, for instance), it becomes
fuel for quantifier instantiation everywhere.

The transpiler decides how much of the surrounding code to bring into a module by how it follows
imports:

- **Shallow** (the default): resolve only the imports the checked file names directly; leave the
  imports *those* modules make in turn as bare placeholders, contributing neither bodies nor contracts.
- **Deep** (`--deep`): resolve imports recursively — the neighbours, their neighbours, and so on — the
  entire transitive closure, every resolved function arriving as a `val` whose contract joins the
  module's logical context.

`--deep` is one all-or-nothing switch; it cannot be aimed at a single dependency. Turned on to reach
the codec, it also resolved everything the filesystem class transitively rests on. The consequence is
not new verification conditions — the imports arrive as bodyless `val`s, which carry no
weakest-precondition obligations of their own — but a sharply larger set of **in-scope hypotheses and
declared symbols**, many of them quantified, now visible to the SMT solver at goals that previously did
not see them.

## Why a larger context makes each goal harder

The intuition that adding proven pieces costs only their own checking is wrong for this kind of proof,
and the reason is specific to SMT discharge of weakest-precondition conditions.

When Alt-Ergo or Z3 works on one sub-goal, the entire in-scope context travels with it. The solver
does not narrow its attention to the hypotheses relevant to that goal; the quantifier-instantiation
engine considers, in principle, *every* universally-quantified hypothesis and axiom in scope, choosing
ground terms (via triggers/E-matching) to instantiate them, and each instantiation produces new ground
facts that can trigger still further instantiations. The size of this instantiation search — and the
number of uninterpreted symbols and theory atoms the combined theory solvers must juggle — grows with
how much is in scope. A goal that closed quickly against a handful of relevant facts can, against a
context swollen by hundreds of additional quantified contracts, explode into an instantiation space the
solver cannot exhaust within thirty seconds, and so returns *Unknown* or *Timeout* where it once
returned *Valid*.

Because that enlarged context is carried into *every* goal, the effect is not additive but **compounding
across the whole module**: a handful of newly-reachable, quantifier-bearing contracts can quietly inflate
the per-goal instantiation cost of thousands of unrelated sub-goals. The expense of `--deep` is therefore
not the cost of proving the newly-reachable code — that code contributes no goals — but the cost of
having injected its *quantified presence* into the shared context against which every existing goal is
already being solved.

## The origin of the complexity

Three facts combine.

First, the depth of import resolution is **binary** — shallow or fully recursive, with nothing between.
There is no way to resolve one named dependency fully and leave the rest as placeholders, so the only
mechanism that reaches the codec is the one that reaches the entire closure.

Second, the os subsystem sits on a **broad transitive base**: the filesystem class rests on a clock, a
shared world model, path utilities, and their own dependencies in turn. The set of everything os
ultimately reaches is far larger than the short list it names directly, and deep resolution exposes all
of it at once as in-scope contracts.

Third, and underneath both, is the **cost model of SMT-discharged, weakest-precondition verification**:
the price of a module is not the sum of its functions checked alone, because the quantified hypotheses
in the shared context are re-paid — through fresh instantiation work — at every single goal. This is the
same phenomenon observed from the opposite side elsewhere in this code: a codec function that Alt-Ergo
closes in seconds when it stands nearly alone takes many minutes inside the full filesystem module,
*its own definition unchanged*, purely because its goals are now solved against a far larger context of
quantified facts. Deep resolution is the operation that enlarges that context.

## The irony, and the way out

The codec was moved into its own file precisely to make the checked module **smaller** — to discharge
the heavy codec conditions once, in a sparse context where Alt-Ergo closes them in seconds, and let the
rest of the system import only a thin contract about the codec's behaviour. `--deep` does the opposite:
by following the new dependency the only way it can, it re-injects not just the codec's contract but the
whole transitive closure's contracts into the one module, re-crowding the context the separation was
meant to thin. The bloat is thus the collision between a goal of *separation* and a tool that only knows
how to *unify*.

The remedy is not "less deep" but "not deep at all": arrange the imports so the codec is a **direct**
neighbour of the checked file, pulled in by the ordinary shallow mechanism as a single `val` carrying
only its narrow contract, while every other dependency stays a placeholder exactly as before. The
module's logical context then grows by one slender contract rather than by an entire closure, the
per-goal instantiation cost is essentially unchanged, and the heavy weakest-precondition conditions of
the codec's body are discharged where they are cheap — alone, in their own file.
